Slide 1 · Title

A cancelled flight shouldn't cost you the trip

0:10

Airlines are very good at detecting disruptions. The experience that follows is queues and phone lines. SkyConnect is a proof of concept for IndoAir: recovery designed around the passenger.

Slide 2 · The problem

The numbers

0:15

Six to nine percent of flights are disrupted on any given day. When it happens, the passenger's options are a forty-minute queue, an unknown hold time, or waiting at the gate. Seventy-three percent would rather handle it themselves when given comparable options.

Slide 3 · Priya

09:35, cancelled

0:10

Priya, Bengaluru to Singapore, opening a workshop at nine tomorrow morning. At 09:35 her flight is cancelled. One notification takes her straight in. No login, no queue.

Slide 4 · The thesis

Deciding is the hard part

0:25

Here is what this iteration is really about. The rebooking transaction takes about ninety seconds. Passengers still queue for forty minutes, because the counter is where a human helps them decide. So SkyConnect treats the decision as the product: one recommended next step, four other paths always in reach, and the honesty a stressed person needs to commit on her own. Let me show you.

Slide 5 · Live demo

Priya's four minutes

1:15

[Dashboard] This is live; you can open it on your phone. The first screen apologises, shows six things the system already did on its own, and puts her one decision above the fold: fly today. [The question] Before showing flights, it asks the one thing the airline cannot look up: what matters most. For Priya, the nine o'clock opening. [Options] Three flights, each labelled with its consequence. Notice the recommendation is deliberately not the earliest arrival. The earliest needs a forty-minute connection, and if that slips she misses the opening entirely. The recommendation is the late direct that puts her in the hotel a full night before. [Scarcity] Seat counts are real and timestamped, never a pressure tactic. [Hold] She can hold the seat for ten minutes, go make a call, and come back. [Confirm] Confirming shows no price and no card, because she is only ever offered her own fare class. [Tell] And recovery ends by drafting the message to the person expecting her. Her total time: under four minutes.

If the demo stalls, the index on the left of the prototype page jumps to any screen.

Slide 6 · Two states

Before vs after

0:20

That is the difference between these two states. On the left, today: fragmented updates, no single source of truth, compensation you ask for and hope. On the right: one dashboard, one guided next action, the passenger in control from the first tap. One feels like a waiting room. The other feels like someone is already handling it.

Slide 7 · Outcomes

Four targets

0:25

Four measurable targets. Recovery in under four minutes of the passenger's own time, where today the queue alone averages forty. Self-service adoption, with seventy-three percent already telling us they want it. One dashboard replacing three to five disconnected touchpoints. And lower counter and call-centre pressure at peak disruption, which is where the operational cost sits. These are design targets; the POC exists to earn the pilot that measures them.

Slide 8 · Close

The agentic layer, and the close

0:30

Everything you saw the system do, it did on its own. Before Priya opened the link, it had classified the disruption, protected a backup seat, issued her benefits, and searched every alternative; while she decided, it kept watching availability, and it escalates only complexity to staff. That agentic layer is the innovation this iteration adds over the last one. It is built on a reusable design system over standard Amadeus APIs, so it scales beyond one route and one scenario. To close: SkyConnect turns the most stressful moment of a passenger's journey into a guided, transparent, self-service recovery. Happy to take questions.
