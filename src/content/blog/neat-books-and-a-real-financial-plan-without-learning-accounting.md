Most founders I know keep their money in four or five places: a business account, a personal account, a credit card or two, and a few friends who owe them something. Nobody wants to be the person who reconciles all of that every month, so it does not get done. Then tax season arrives, or a big decision, and nobody can say what the business actually earns or what living costs.

This week my assistant started doing that work. I am not an accountant and I did not write any accounting software. What I did was set up a few habits and write a short list of rules. This post is about what that makes possible, and how you would set it up yourself.

## What becomes possible

**Every cost lands in the right place.** If you run more than one project, each tool, subscription and domain belongs to one of them. Paid from one card, the costs still end up in the right project's books, because a rule says Domain A belongs to Project 1 and Hosting B belongs to Project 2.

**Shared money stops living in your head.** A dinner split five ways, a trip where friends paid for different things, a family member who uses your card: each of these gets recorded once with the share that is really yours. Your monthly spending then shows what you spent, not what passed through your card.

**You find money that went missing.** A refund that never arrived, a charge that appeared twice, a bill that bounced and came back: with every line read and sorted, these stand out. In one month I found a cancelled booking where the refund had stopped halfway between the airline and the booking site.

**You see your real margin.** Money in, minus what you spend on living, minus what the business spends on tools, divided by money in. One number per month. Mine turned out to be lumpy: a month with a big payment looks great, and the three months around it look bad. Seeing that changes how you plan, because your reserve has to cover the thin months.

**Planning gets real numbers.** Once the books are tidy you know your typical monthly spend, your typical income, and how uneven that income is. Questions like how long a gap you can survive, or what a cut in one category would save over a year, have answers from your own data, not guesses.

**Targets you can actually track.** I picked one category that was quietly growing (for me it was food delivery) and set a limit per month. The report now tells me where I stand against it.

## How to set it up

You do not need to build this all at once. Here is the order I would follow.

1. **Give the assistant one inbox.** Create a separate email address that only the assistant reads. Set a filter in your own mailbox so the monthly statements from your bank and card forward there automatically. Statements are the raw material for everything else.

2. **Write your rules once.** Open a plain sheet with three columns: the words that appear on a statement line, the category it belongs to, and a note. "Swiggy" means food delivery. "Hosting company" means Project 2. A new merchant shows up as unlabelled, you say what it is, and the rule is added. The first month needs the most of your attention. After that it shrinks.

3. **Insist on a check.** Every statement lists an opening and closing balance. Make the assistant confirm that the lines it read add up to the closing balance, and stop and tell you if they do not. This one rule is why I trust the numbers.

4. **Tell it things in plain words.** Money arrives in an account the assistant cannot see, or you split a bill. Send a message ("received 40,000 from a client today", "the Goa dinner was split five ways"). It records the item in the right file and confirms in one line. A message from your phone is enough; there is no form to fill in.

5. **Get one report a month.** Have it send you a short summary after each statement cycle: money in, spending by category, anything unlabelled, and the margin against the last few months. Read it once. Reply with the corrections.

## Guardrails worth copying

Passwords stay in a file that only you save. The assistant never asks for them in chat. It can send email only to your own addresses, and anything for another person comes to you as a draft. Sensitive money files stay on your own machines. They do not go into public tools or shared documents. A job that does not match the numbers stops and says so, instead of guessing.

## Where to start this week

Pick the one account that causes you the most trouble. Forward its last three statements to a fresh inbox, write down the ten merchants you see most, and give each a category. That is enough for a first useful month. The planning part comes free once the numbers are there.
