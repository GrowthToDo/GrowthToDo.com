Product market fit is a useful phrase, but it hides more than it reveals.

Over the years of working on internet native products and services, one pattern keeps repeating. Teams are not really chasing some abstract PMF milestone. What they are actually doing is continuously adjusting a small set of concrete variables in an attempt to make the business viable.

That leads to a clearer framing: AODM Fit.

Audience. Offering. Distribution. Money.

The problem is that PMF compresses too many moving parts into a single term. It creates endless debate about whether a product has "fit" without clearly identifying what is broken.

AODM Fit forces that precision.

No matter how big and complex a company looks on the outside, it is built on the same basic principles, the ones Tobi Lutke laid out:

Make something people want. Make something people want and are willing to pay for. Make something enough people want, are willing to pay for, and can be sustainably delivered profitably.

Only the last stage qualifies as a business.

Everything before that is a project.

Break that progression apart and the abstraction of Audience, Offering, Distribution, and Money falls out of it naturally.

| | |
|---|---|
| Make something enough people want | **Audience**, Pain/Joy Point, **Offering** & Market size |
| are willing to pay for | **Money**, Business model, Revenue generation, Payment collection |
| can be sustainably delivered profitably | **Distribution** & Profitability |
| Investable | Capital is available and willing |

## Breaking down AODM Fit

Audience answers a single question: which group of people are we serving?

Offering answers: what is the value we're adding to that audience?

Distribution answers: how do we get the offering to our audience?

Money answers: how do we extract value, and does the business model, the revenue generation, the payment collection actually work?

If even one of these is weak, the entire structure suffers.

A product, no matter how big a pain or joy point it solves, without stellar distribution is a dud. The company won't even take off from the ground.

No matter how stellar the distribution and no matter how many dollars are thrown at it, if a product doesn't solve a real pain point or joy point, it's as good as dead from the beginning. The company will die a slow death.

## A real example: Pupilfirst, 2013 to 2021

I'll use my own company, Pupilfirst, to illustrate what AODM fit actually looks like when you tinker with it over years. I'm deliberately excluding future plans or second-degree definitions here. There are nuances this leaves out. But at its heart, this is where things stood.

Here's the 2013 snapshot, back when we started as Startup Village:

| | Primary Definition in 2013 |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | Anyone who wants to start a startup in India. We pretty much didn't say No to anyone who came to us. |
| **Offering** | Free office space in Kochi, fast internet connection and unfiltered access to the community. Virtual incubation arrangement was available for the ones we couldn't physically accommodate. The ecosystem offered network effects of its own. |
| **Distribution** | Apply directly on the website [www.startupvillage.in](https://www.startupvillage.in) |
| **Money** | DST Grants, Funds raised into ITIH non-profit, GoK gave the space at extremely subsidized cost. |

And here's where AODM fit stood by June 2021, as Pupilfirst:

| Term | Meaning | Primary Definition - June 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | Which group of people are we serving? | Students who are currently doing their B.Tech degrees in colleges across India, especially in Tier 2, Tier 3 colleges, mostly in private deemed Universities |
| **Offering** | What is the value we're adding to the 'Audience'? | We launch and run industry driven courses in emerging technologies for the B.Tech students who are desirous and inclined to learn. This will help them get much better jobs in the industry. WD course is our top offering. |
| **Distribution** | How to get the offering to our Audience? | We get these courses to students by launching them as credited electives by partnering with the Universities/Colleges. |
| **Money** | How do we extract value? | Costing is done on a per student basis and our revenue is collected by invoicing the Universities. Operationally, we are funded by investors like 3one4. |

Our big shifts from 2013 to 2021 were all about tinkering with the form factor to get closer to true AODM fit. We're nearing it. We're still not really there yet.

## AODM Fit is part science, part art

A product in its very early stages of AODM fit needs very loose structure, so you can maximize your freedom to explore off-shoot tangents based on instinct. Just enough structure to keep things sane, but not so much that it becomes stifling.

![An idea leads through several tangents before arriving at an outcome, illustrating how off-shoot exploration eventually converges](/images/blog/aodm-idea-tangent-outcome-diagram.png)

Even the learning required to advance the cause in the early stage happens in a very unstructured manner.

![Structured learning moves in a straight line from start to finish with one breakthrough. Unstructured learning loops and doubles back through many breakthroughs before reaching finish](/images/blog/aodm-structured-vs-unstructured-learning.png)

That balance between structure and unstructure isn't static. It's a dynamic, evolving thing. As you approach a public release of an iteration, you need to become much more structured. People who understand this dynamic balance it well and produce great creative work. When you work as a team, you need people with the creative flair and people who bring in discipline.

The early days of AODM fit, especially when it's just Audience and Offering, are mostly chaotic. It's in that zone that I've found I'm able to thrive most.

## Why this framing matters

Without a clear AODM lens, teams often fix the wrong problems.

They polish features when the audience is wrong.
They spend on marketing when the offering is weak.
They chase revenue models before distribution exists.

AODM Fit provides a diagnostic tool. It helps you ask better questions earlier and avoid being misled by surface level traction.

It does not guarantee success. But it reduces the chance of building something impressive that never becomes real.

That alone makes it worth adopting.
