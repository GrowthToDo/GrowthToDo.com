I named this GrowthToDo for a reason.

I think about growth — at any stage, in any business — as a sequencing problem. Not a strategy problem. Not a vision problem. A sequencing problem.

Here is the mental model: imagine everything you need to do to get from where you are to where you want to be as a list. A very long list. Maybe a million items. Every conversation you need to have, every experiment you need to run, every thing you need to ship, every decision you need to make.

That list exists whether you can see it or not.

The question is never "is there a path?" There is always a path. The question is: do you have the right items on your list, and are you doing them in the right order?

## Most growth problems are sequencing problems

When a founder tells me they are stuck, it is almost never because the market does not exist or the idea is fundamentally broken. It is usually one of two things:

They are doing the right things in the wrong order. Or they are doing the wrong things entirely.

Both of these are to-do list problems.

Spending six months building infrastructure before validating demand is a sequencing error. Hiring a marketing agency before nailing the offer is a sequencing error. Raising money before knowing what you would do with it is a sequencing error.

The fix is not a new strategy. It is a better to-do list, in a better order.

## Execution is underrated

There is a tendency in startup culture to romanticise insight. The big idea. The contrarian take. The unique angle.

Those things matter. But they matter a lot less than people think, and execution matters a lot more.

Two founders can start with the same insight. The one who sequences their actions better — who does the right things first, who does not waste months on premature work — will win. Almost every time.

I have seen this pattern repeat across every business I have been involved in since 2013. The bottleneck is rarely the idea. It is almost always the to-do list.

## What this means in practice

When I work with founders, the first thing I try to do is get a clear picture of their current to-do list — implicit or explicit. What are they actually spending time on? What are they avoiding? What do they believe needs to happen before other things can happen?

Then we question the order. We ask: what is the one thing that, if done, makes everything else easier or unnecessary? That goes to the top. Everything else gets pushed down or removed.

This sounds simple. It is not easy. It requires being honest about what you are avoiding and why. It requires letting go of work you have already done if it is no longer the right next step.

But when you get the sequence right, momentum builds fast. Each action creates the conditions for the next one. Progress compounds.

## Hence, GrowthToDo

That is the idea behind the name. Growth is not magic. It is not luck. It is not just vision.

It is the right to-dos, in the right order, executed.

That is it. That is the whole philosophy. Everything else is a footnote.
